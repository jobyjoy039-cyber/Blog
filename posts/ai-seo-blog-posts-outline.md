#### H3 1.1: The Three Structural Reasons AI Content Fails to Rank (It's Not a Penalty)

**Content brief:** Cover the three failure modes:
1. Topical surface coverage — AI writes to the middle of the bell curve, covering what everyone covers, so it carries zero differentiation signal

3. Keyword integration without intent matching — hitting keyword density while missing the actual search intent of the query

**Writer note:** Use the phrase "Google doesn't penalize AI content — it rewards *helpful* content, and helpful content has characteristics that most raw AI output lacks." This is the mechanism pivot.

#### H3 2.1: Phase 1 — Keyword and Intent Mapping (Do This Before You Open Any AI Tool)

**Content brief:**
- Start with the SERP, not the keyword tool. Analyze the top 5 results for your target query to classify intent: informational, commercial, navigational, transactional
- Identify the content format Google is rewarding: listicle, how-to, comparison, pillar page
- Note the word count range of ranking pages (use an SEO tool — Ahrefs, Semrush, or free Detailed.io extension)
- Extract the PAA questions directly from Google — these become H3 subheadings
- Output of Phase 1: a one-page "intent brief" with format, angle, PAA questions, competitor gaps

**Writer note:** Emphasize that skipping Phase 1 is the single most common reason AI posts don't rank — you are handing the AI a prompt with no strategic targeting.

#### H3 2.2: Phase 2 — Building the Prompt Stack (Not One Prompt — A System)

**Content brief:**
- Introduce the concept of a "prompt stack" — a sequence of 3–4 prompts that builds the article in layers rather than generating it all at once
- Prompt 1: Outline generation prompt (structured, role-assigned, with intent brief baked in)
- Prompt 2: Section drafting prompt (one section at a time, with word count and subheading constraints)
- Prompt 3: EEAT injection prompt (asks the AI to add specific examples, data, named sources)
- Prompt 4: Transition and flow prompt (asks AI to connect sections and vary sentence rhythm)
- Include actual example prompt structure for Prompt 1 (as a formatted code block)

**Featured snippet opportunity:** "What prompts should I use for SEO articles?" — this section's example prompt structure can be captured.

**Visual element suggestion:** A prompt template card — formatted like a UI card, showing the anatomy of the Phase 2 Prompt 1 with labeled fields (Role, Context, Output Format, Constraints).

#### H3 2.3: Phase 3 — First-Draft Generation and the "Skeleton First" Rule

**Content brief:**
- Generate the H2/H3 skeleton first, review it against the intent brief before generating any body copy
- Approve, revise, or restructure headings before drafting — rewriting headings after body copy is written is expensive
- Generate body copy section by section, not all at once
- After each section: immediately flag what needs human addition (stats, quotes, personal experience, brand examples)

**Writer note:** Frame the "skeleton first" rule as the equivalent of an architect approving blueprints before construction. The analogy makes it memorable and shareable.

#### H3 2.4: Phase 4 — The Human Layer (Where Rankings Are Actually Won)

**Content brief:**
This is the most important section for differentiation. The human layer is not editing — it is *adding*. Five specific things to add:
1. A named real-world example or brand case (not a fictional or generic "company X")
2. One original data point or proprietary observation (internal analytics, a test you ran, a client result)
3. A dissenting opinion or nuance that the AI flattened ("Most guides say X, but in practice...")
4. At least one specific tool recommendation with an honest take (not a generic list)
5. A first-person qualifier that signals the author has done this (past tense experience statements)

**Writer note:** Explain WHY each addition matters as an EEAT signal — Google's quality raters look for these markers. This is the mechanism-level explanation the audience wants.

**Visual element suggestion:** A "Human Layer Checklist" — 5 checkboxes with one-line descriptions. This is also a natural content upgrade element (downloadable PDF version).

**Internal link zone — H3 2.4:** Link to your EEAT guide with anchor text "E-E-A-T signals."

#### H3 2.5: Phase 5 — On-Page SEO Pass (The Structured Checklist)

**Content brief:**
Run a discrete on-page pass *after* the human layer, not during drafting. Cover:
- Primary keyword in H1, first 100 words, one H2, and meta description (not stuffed — one occurrence per zone)
- Semantic keyword integration: use an NLP tool (Clearscope, Surfer, or Frase) to identify missing semantic terms and insert naturally
- Internal linking: identify 3–5 contextual opportunities and link to relevant existing posts
- Image alt text: descriptive, keyword-adjacent, not keyword-stuffed
- URL: short, keyword-containing, hyphenated
- Schema markup: add Article schema; if FAQs are present, add FAQPage schema
- Meta description: 140–155 chars, includes primary keyword and a clear value proposition

**Featured snippet opportunity:** This checklist structure can be captured as a featured snippet for "on-page SEO checklist for blog posts."

**H4 2.5.1: How to Use Clearscope (or Surfer) to Find Missing Semantic Terms**

Content: Brief walkthrough — paste draft, review graded terms list, identify C/D grade terms that are topically relevant, integrate 3–5 naturally. Emphasize that the goal is topical completeness, not score-chasing.

#### H3 2.6: Phase 6 — The Post-Publish Audit (Most People Skip This, Which Is Why They Fail)

**Content brief:**
The post-publish audit is the article's most unique section — competitors don't cover this. Cover:
- Set a 90-day calendar reminder after every AI-assisted post
- Connect to Google Search Console (GSC) and check: impressions, average position, which queries triggered the page
- If impressions exist but CTR is low: the problem is the title tag or meta description — iterate
- If average position is 15–30 and stuck: the content depth or EEAT layer is insufficient — go back and enrich
- If zero impressions after 90 days: indexing issue or the page is being devalued — run a content quality audit
- Introduce the concept of "content decay" and refresh cadence

**Visual element suggestion:** A 2x3 diagnostic table — Column 1: "What GSC Shows," Column 2: "What It Means," Column 3: "What to Do Next." This is highly shareable and a featured snippet candidate.

**Featured snippet opportunity:** "Why is my blog post getting impressions but no clicks?" — the diagnostic table targets this query.

#### H3 6.1: Can AI-Written Content Actually Rank on Google?

**Answer brief:** Yes — with the workflow above. The distinction Google draws is between content made to rank (thin, generic, unhelpful) vs. content made to help people (specific, accurate, experienced). AI-generated text that has been human-enriched with EEAT signals, accurate information, and genuine specificity ranks. Raw, unedited AI output rarely does.

#### H3 6.2: Does Google Penalize AI-Written Content?

**Answer brief:** Google does not penalize content for being AI-generated. It penalizes content that is low-quality, unhelpful, or manipulative — characteristics that often describe *unedited* AI output but not AI-assisted content produced with the workflow in this guide. Cite: Google Search Central guidance, updated 2024.

#### H3 6.3: How Do I Add E-E-A-T to AI Content?

**Answer brief:** Four concrete additions: (1) a first-hand experience statement in the opening, (2) at least one original data point or named real-world example, (3) a specific tool or resource recommendation with a direct opinion, and (4) a complete author bio with credentials and linked profile. These four additions shift the EEAT signal from neutral to positive.

#### H3 6.4: What Prompts Should I Use for SEO Blog Articles?

**Answer brief:** Use a four-prompt stack, not a single prompt. Prompt 1: generate the outline with role, context, format, and constraints specified. Prompts 2–4: generate each major section separately with a running context note. The final prompt: a revision pass for flow and transition quality. Refer reader to Phase 2 in the workflow above.

#### H3 6.5: How Long Should an AI Blog Post Be to Rank?

**Answer brief:** Match the word count range of the top 5 ranking results for your target query — not a universal number. For most informational how-to queries, 2,000–4,000 words is typical. The signal is coverage completeness, not raw length. A 1,500-word post that answers the question completely outranks a 4,000-word post that pads.
