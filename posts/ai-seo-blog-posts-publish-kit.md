## Featured Snippet Answers

**Query: "How do I use AI to write SEO blog posts?"**
> Use a six-phase workflow: (1) map keyword and search intent before opening any AI tool, (2) build a four-prompt stack rather than a single article prompt, (3) generate a skeleton outline first and approve it before drafting, (4) inject the human layer — real-world examples, original data, documented failures, and first-person observations, (5) run an on-page SEO pass including a semantic term audit with Clearscope or Surfer, (6) run a Google Search Console diagnostic at 30, 60, and 90 days post-publish.

**Query: "Does Google penalize AI-written content?"**
> Google does not penalize content for being AI-written. It penalizes content that is unhelpful, produced primarily to manipulate rankings, or lacks genuine value — characteristics that often describe unedited AI output but not AI-assisted content produced with editorial oversight. The standard is helpfulness, not authorship method.

**Query: "How long should an AI blog post be to rank?"**
> Match the content length of the top five ranking pages for your target keyword. For informational queries, this is typically 1,500 to 3,500 words. Padding beyond the intent-appropriate length dilutes topical density and works against quality signals.

## AI Overview Summary

AI can write SEO blog posts that rank when it operates inside a structured six-phase workflow: intent mapping, prompt stacking, skeleton-first drafting, human-layer injection (E-E-A-T signals), on-page SEO pass, and post-publish GSC auditing. Google does not penalize AI-written content — it penalizes unhelpful content regardless of production method. The ranking differentiator is the human layer: first-hand experience, documented failures, specific tool opinions, and original data that AI cannot generate from a prompt alone. Sites using AI-assisted content grow organic traffic 29% faster than those without it (Ahrefs, 2025), but purely AI-generated content accounts for just 4.6% of top-ranking pages — confirming that the workflow, not the tool, determines ranking outcomes.
